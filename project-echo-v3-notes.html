Title — "echo"

Walk to the front. Let the slide sit in silence for two full seconds. Do not start immediately. Then begin, slowly.

Every business is built on promises. Promises made in contracts, in emails, in phone calls. Promises about what a customer will pay, what discount they agreed to, what happens when it is time to renew. These promises are the commercial foundation of every relationship we have.

And right now — we are losing them. Not in one big failure. Quietly. One missed email. One discount that silently runs past its expiry. One conversation that only one person ever knew about — and they left six months ago.

Project Echo is here to fix that. For good.

Move to the next slide immediately, without adding anything else.

Slide 2 of 13
The Problem — One Question. Five Systems.

Point at each system on the screen as you name it. Move your hand deliberately from left to right across the five boxes.

A renewal question arrives — something simple. Can this customer keep their discount? And here is what actually happens today. Open HubSpot. Find a note that says: see email. Open the email — it is a thread 48 messages deep with no resolution. Find the PDF contract, buried in section seven. Find a Slack message from someone who already left — just to clarify the discount is temp btw — which is, of course, legally meaningless. And finally, find a spreadsheet called v3-FINAL-v2-UPDATED-USE-THIS.

Five systems. Manual reconstruction. Every single time. And here is the thing — this is not a process failure. It is a structural problem. Commercial intent was never designed to be captured. It was assumed someone would remember. That assumption is breaking us.

Slide 3 of 13
The Question — The Moment of Truth

Make eye contact with the room before you speak. Ask the question directly, not rhetorically — you actually want people to think about it.

Right now — today — for any of our active clients — can anyone tell me with confidence whether their current discount was agreed to be permanent, or temporary?

Pause. Four full seconds of silence. Let it be uncomfortable. Do not fill it.

If the person who ran that deal is in this room, maybe yes. If they are on holiday — probably not. If they left six months ago — definitely not. This is the question behind every renewal conversation we have. And right now, the honest answer is: we do not know. Not quickly, not safely. That is a revenue risk and a legal risk every single time a renewal comes around. We need something smarter than hoping someone remembers. We need it to be structural.

Slide 4 of 13
Why This Matters — The Cost and the Risk

And it is not just inefficiency — every one of these problems has a direct financial consequence. Discounts running past their agreed scope is revenue walking out the door silently. Verbal commitments never captured are legal disputes waiting to happen. And when the person who holds all the commercial context for a client leaves — we do not just lose an employee. We lose every promise, every nuance, every conversation. Gone.

Here is the practical test: think of the one person on your team who knows the most about your client accounts. Now imagine they are on holiday. Or they have just handed in their notice. What happens to your renewals?

Project Echo is the answer. It converts that personal knowledge into something the whole organisation can access, any time.

Slide 5 of 13
Introducing Echo — Two Modes

Project Echo works in two modes. The first is Sales mode — it helps your sales team capture deal terms correctly before the agreement is ever sent. It runs checks, flags missing clauses, and drafts the right email — so that twelve months later, renewal is easy because the groundwork was done right.

The second is Renewal mode — it gives your CS team complete commercial intelligence at every renewal: what was agreed, what has changed, what the risks are, and what needs to happen.

Let me show you both.

Advance the slide with purpose — you are moving into the demo section now.

Slide 6 of 13
Echo Sales — Deal Capture

Think about the moment a deal is won. There is a price agreed, a discount given, a contract about to be sent. How much of the commercial reasoning behind that agreement is actually captured before it goes out?

Echo intercepts right here — before the agreement leaves your hands. The sales rep enters the deal details, and Echo immediately reviews the contract language.

Gesture to the middle column — the amber Contract Warnings panel.

In this case, Clause 4.2 has a payment term issue — it says payment upfront, but our subscription model requires annual billing in advance. And Clause 7.1 has no discount duration, which is the most common source of renewal disputes. Echo points to the exact clause and tells you exactly what the new wording should be. Nothing leaves your hands until it is right.

Slide 7 of 13
Echo Drafts — Email and Contract Clause

This is where Echo becomes genuinely powerful. From the same commercial data — the same deal terms — Echo produces two different documents in two different registers.

Gesture left, then right.

On the left, the confirmation email: clear, warm, professional, written for a person. On the right, the contract clause: formal, precise, legally sound, written for a document. Both capture the same commercial intent — the pricing, the discount duration, the uplift mechanism. The email says it in plain English. The contract says it in enforceable terms.

And both are ready instantly. No drafting from scratch. No risk of one contradicting the other.

Slide 9 of 13
Echo Renewal — Current State (Acme Health)

Now here is Acme Health — a client where Echo was not in place from day one. The commercial summary tells the full story: a 20% discount applied, but it was only ever meant to be temporary. The risk flags show us what was missed — the discount duration was never formally documented. And notice the renewal term column.

Point to the rightmost column.

This contract has a mutual agreement clause, not a simple auto-renew. That means renewal is not automatic. Both parties need to confirm it in writing. Echo interprets that clause and — crucially — also reads the tone of recent emails to advise on what level of documentation is appropriate. This customer is cooperative, which means a formal letter can work for the renewal itself. But for the pricing change, a signed variation provides the strongest protection. Echo makes that recommendation clear.

If presenting live, demonstrate by updating the lives or discount field. Otherwise, describe it as you point to each section.

Slide 11 of 13
Variation and Template Generation

Echo does not just calculate — it governs the paperwork. And the scope here is broader than just pricing. Any change to subscription term, contract duration, payment structure, or termination conditions can require a formal variation.

For Acme Health, the pricing change plus the mutual-agreement renewal clause means a signed variation is required. Echo tells you exactly what it must include, pre-fills your company template, and generates a ready-to-download document for signature.

Point to the right column — the upload zone and download button.

And this works for Sales too — if a customer needs different payment terms from your standard agreement, Echo can amend that template before the deal is signed. The paper trail is complete from day one through every renewal.

Slide 12 of 13
Future Vision — The Platform

The MVP you have just seen covers five core capabilities: deal capture, contract drafting, renewal intelligence, pricing calculation, and variation generation. The platform vision extends this into a full commercial intelligence layer.

Move through this slide at a confident pace — about 30 seconds. The demo has already done the heavy lifting. You are painting a horizon, not pitching features.

Live meeting capture, so Echo joins renewal calls and extracts commitments in real time. A revenue opportunity dashboard, giving you a portfolio view of every account with uplift potential or risk. Automated CRM enrichment, so the right data flows back into HubSpot without manual entry. It is commercial infrastructure.
